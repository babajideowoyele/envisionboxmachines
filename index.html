Machines sphere

Machines as subjects, and as instruments

Robots, models and agents are both things we study with behavioural methods and things we put inside behavioural research. Both carry people: the humans a robot records, the bodies a model learned from, the coder a model replaces.

What this sphere is

Robots, models and agents, in two roles at once. The sphere asks two questions and applies the four pathways to both.

How do we integrate them into behavioural research?

Robots as interaction partners or confederates. Models as annotators and coders. Synthetic humans as stimuli. Models as simulated participants. Models as the instruments inside our own methods.

How do we study them with behavioural research?

Robot and model behaviour measured with the methods used on people: turn-taking, gesture, gaze, synchrony, kinematics. Psychometrics applied to language models. Developmental robotics as a model of how infants learn.

From tools to platform to infrastructure

EnvisionBOX began as a collection of tutorials. The aim is to become a platform, then infrastructure that labs, consortia and funders can build on. Each step is defined by what someone else can rely on, and passing between steps has a gate: criteria from the BOX framework that must be met first.

  1. Now

    Tools

    Runnable methods for studying machines and studying with them.

    • Feature extraction from a robot’s camera and microphone, in the ROS4HRI format
    • Validating a language model as a coder against human coders
    • Coding agent dyads with the same scheme used for human dyads
    • All runnable in Reachy Mini’s simulator, without a robot
    Why The methods exist in pieces across robotics, machine learning and psychology. No shared place runs them side by side.
    • ROS4HRI standard
    • Reachy Mini
  2. Gate A

    Mostly technical. A small team can meet it.

    • Reads and writes a shared format
    • Automated tests and reference data
    • A validation report against human coding
    • Released in citable versions
    • A way for others to contribute back
  3. 2027

    Platform

    Models and methods with their provenance on record.

    • A registry of every model a module uses: training data, licence, consent, AI Act status
    • A model card for each one
    • Claim-to-code audits of methods papers in Behavior Research Methods
    Why Provenance is mostly missing. An audit of popular datasets found licences miscategorised in about two thirds of cases, and the EDPB holds that a model trained on people is not anonymous by default.
    • Longpre et al., Data Provenance Initiative
    • EDPB Opinion 28/2024
    • bioimage.io model specification
  4. Gate B

    Mostly social and institutional. No single lab can meet it.

    • A community data standard
    • An open benchmark with fixed splits
    • Governance and a maintainer rota
    • Funding beyond a single grant
    • Provenance for every result
    • Evidence the measures hold across the populations served
  5. 2028 onward

    Infrastructure

    An audit and provenance service other labs rely on.

    • A reproduction service run with HPI and TU Delft
    • A run log with fingerprints of every input and output
    • A registry other platforms can query
    • Shared governance for what the AI Act allows us to distribute
    Why In psychology, 36% of articles carrying an open-data badge reproduced without the authors’ help. Claim-to-code audits work for machine learning: AuditOwl traced 100 NeurIPS 2025 papers. None exist yet for behavioural science.
    • Hardwicke et al. 2021
    • AuditOwl

Three principles that hold at every stage

From the BOX framework. These keep validity, researcher judgement and methodological diversity intact as tools grow into infrastructure.

P1
Validity as a feature

Every tool ships with a synthetic test signal, a human-coded reference set and a short validation report naming its known failure conditions.

Here: Every module that uses a model as a coder ships a human-coded reliability subset and reports agreement against it. Scripted agent dialogues serve as the synthetic test signal.

P2
Keep the researcher observant

Every derived measure can be viewed against the video it came from, and this view is the default, not an option.

Here: Every model annotation, and every measure of a robot’s behaviour, can be viewed against the recording or transcript it came from.

P3
Plurality by design

Several trackers, features and metrics run through common interfaces, and benchmarks compare them rather than crown one.

Here: Several models, open-weight and others, run through one interface. The registry records what each was trained on and where it holds; it does not endorse one.

Four pathways

Every sphere is built along the same four. Protection is assumed under all of them: masking, consent and data governance are the condition for a method to be listed, not a feature of the sphere.

Participation

On what terms do people take part?
Validity

Does the method measure what it claims, on this population?
Reproducibility

Can someone else get the same result from the same inputs?
Accessibility

Who can use it, who can contribute, who is left out?

Pathway 1

Participation

Every robot corpus and every behaviour model is a human dataset. Seamless Interaction holds over 4,000 participants with no public consent text. Whisper was trained on 680,000 scraped hours. Sapiens on 300 million unreleased images. Two thirds of Hugging Face dataset licences are mislabelled. The EDPB holds that a model trained on people is not anonymous by default. Provenance — what a model learned from, under what terms — is this sphere’s form of masking.

What identifies a subject

None for a machine or a rendered model. Any human interacting with it carries the full human case.

What the law requires

None for the machine or the model.

Why it is hard

There is no trade to make, which is what makes this sphere useful: it is the only one where a method can be measured with nothing hidden.

What this pathway needs

  • A registry that records, per model: licence, training-data licence, whether the training set is available, consent documentation, AI Act status, and whether outputs identify people
  • Datasheets for the openly downloadable HRI corpora, recording platform, licence tier, release form and whether a consent statement exists
  • De-identification as the mandatory last step in robot-view pipelines
  • Terms on which people contribute recordings or compute to model studies

Pathway 2

Validity

Machine coders hold for observable features and group means, and not for inferred constructs: kappa around .5–.6 where human pairs reach .84–.97. Models as simulated participants reproduce means, not variance. A psychometric test built for people has to be re-validated before it says anything about a model. As instruments, the pose, face and speech models every module runs on have to hold for the population they are pointed at.

Pathway 3

Reproducibility

Claim-to-code audits exist for machine learning — AuditOwl traced 100 NeurIPS 2025 papers to their code — and not for behavioural science, where about a third of papers reproduce without the authors and three quarters of archived R files fail on first run. TU Delft’s Digital Competence Centre already issues CODECHECK certificates. This pathway transposes the audit to Behavior Research Methods and submits it to Open Science NL’s replication instrument as a reproduction.

0modules with a pinned environment
0modules with a smoke test
0findings backed by a benchmark
0papers audited claim-to-code

What this pathway needs

  • A claim-to-code audit pilot on Behavior Research Methods papers: static trace, R-aware gates, synthetic stand-ins where data cannot be shared, paired with a CODECHECK certificate
  • A run log: module, version, parameters, input and output fingerprints
  • A model card for every model a module depends on
  • Weekly headless execution of every module

Sources

  • AuditOwl
  • CODECHECK
  • de Bruïne, Hillebrink, de Man, 2026

Pathway 4

Accessibility

Open hardware now costs under $500: Reachy Mini is $399–499, Furhat about €5,000 a year, and Pepper is end-of-life after Aldebaran’s 2025 insolvency. WebGPU has shipped in all four major browsers since November 2025, so pose models run in the browser. The AI Act’s open-source carve-out does not cover Article 5 or Article 50 systems, so what this platform may distribute is stated per module.

What this pathway needs

  • Reachy Mini plus its MuJoCo simulation as reference hardware, so a module runs without a robot
  • In-browser inference, with its limits stated: weights still download, kinematics still identify
  • A governance checklist every module links to: anonymity assessment, Article 50 marking, reporting items
  • A written AI Act position before any emotion-recognition module is listed
